Who Am I

My name is Arthur Holsbeke. I'm a third-year Applied Computer Science student specialising in Software Engineering at PXL University of Applied Sciences in Hasselt, Belgium. I'm 21 years old, and I've spent the better part of the last three years learning how to build software that matters.

Where I Come From

I grew up in the Limburg region of Belgium, in a household where technology was always present but never particularly emphasized. My interest in computing started early – not from a specific "eureka" moment, but from a gradual accumulation of curiosity. I took things apart to understand how they worked. I asked why websites looked the way they did. I wrote my first lines of code at around 16, building a rudimentary webpage to display sports scores for fun. It wasn't good. It was HTML tables all the way down. But it worked, and that feeling – the feeling of having made something function through logic and intention – never really went away.

Choosing Applied Computer Science at PXL felt like the natural next step. The program offered the right blend of practical engineering skills and theoretical foundations, and being located in Hasselt meant I could stay close to a region with a growing tech community that I've come to appreciate deeply, particularly through events like the Tech & Meet series.

What Drives Me

At the core, I'm motivated by problem solving. I genuinely enjoy the process of encountering a complex problem, breaking it down into smaller components, and building a solution that is both functional and elegant. This applies as much to a tricky algorithmic challenge as it does to a user interface that refuses to lay out properly. The fact that software engineering offers an endless supply of interesting problems to solve is, frankly, a gift.

I'm also deeply interested in the craft dimension of software development – the difference between code that merely works and code that is well-structured, readable, and maintainable. Clean code isn't an academic luxury; it's what separates a codebase that a team can confidently evolve from one that becomes a liability. I hold that belief strongly and try to apply it in everything I build.

Outside of Code

When I'm not building something or studying, I spend time following developments in the tech industry – reading engineering blogs, following open-source projects, and keeping tabs on the broader landscape of tools and frameworks. I'm also a keen follower of motorsport, and I find interesting parallels between competitive racing and software engineering: both reward preparation, precision, adaptability under pressure, and a relentless drive to improve.

I'm a social person who values collaboration and learns well in team settings. The projects where I've grown the most have almost always been the ones where I worked closely with others – where code review, disagreement, and shared problem-solving pushed everyone to a higher standard.

Where I'm Headed

I'm currently preparing for my internship period, which represents the culmination of three years of study and the beginning of my professional career. I'm looking for an environment where I can contribute meaningfully, learn from experienced engineers, and work on software that solves real problems for real users. I'm particularly interested in fullstack web development roles where I can apply my Angular and Spring Boot knowledge in a production context.

Long-term, I want to grow into a software architect role – someone who can design scalable, maintainable systems and help teams build with clarity and purpose. I know that's a journey of many years, and I'm genuinely excited to take the next step.
