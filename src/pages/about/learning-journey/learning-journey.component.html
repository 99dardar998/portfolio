Reflections

The biggest lesson of my learning journey isn't technical. It's the understanding that the feeling of not knowing enough never entirely goes away – and that's not a problem to solve, it's a feature of the field. Every engineer I've spoken with has confirmed this: the best ones are perpetually learning, perpetually encountering the edge of what they know, and perpetually curious about what's beyond it.

I've made peace with that. More than peace – I find it motivating. There is always something interesting to learn, always a better way to do something, always a perspective I haven't considered. Software engineering is the right career for someone who is fundamentally curious and fundamentally uncomfortable standing still.

What I'm most proud of from my three years of study isn't any specific project or grade. It's the shift in how I think about problems. I approach things differently now – more structurally, more skeptically, and with more appreciation for the complexity that lies beneath simple-looking surfaces. That, I think, is what education is actually for.
