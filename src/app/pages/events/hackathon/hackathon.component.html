Overview

For this hackathon, a friend and I decided to team up and participate in a DevOps-related challenge. The main objective was not to develop a complex application but rather to learn how to deploy and manage a website on a server. Using an SSH connection, we had to access a remote server and configure it to host a website through Nginx. While the website itself needed some content, the actual focus of the challenge was on hosting, server management, and the proper use of resources rather than on software development.

My teammate was not a software engineering student but a cybersecurity student. This made for an interesting dynamic — we came at problems from different angles and had to help each other understand unfamiliar concepts. Because we already had a strong friendship, we worked well together and managed to keep the atmosphere relaxed and enjoyable, even when things did not go according to plan.

The Challenge

The challenge required us to access a remote server via SSH and configure it to host a website using Nginx. The emphasis was firmly on the operational side: server setup, resource management, and correct configuration rather than building sophisticated software. From the very beginning, it became clear that we were not the most experienced team for this particular challenge. Neither of us had much practical experience with server administration or DevOps practices, so we often had to figure things out as we went along, learning through trial and error.

Obstacles

Every step of the challenge presented something new to overcome. Configuring the server, understanding how Nginx works, and troubleshooting SSH connection issues all required us to research and problem-solve in real time. Neither of us had a strong background in this area, which meant every error message was a learning opportunity rather than a familiar bump in the road.

The cross-discipline nature of our team also added an interesting layer. My cybersecurity teammate approached problems differently than I did as a software engineering student, which sometimes meant we talked past each other before finding a shared understanding. In the end, that difference turned out to be more of a strength than a weakness.

Lessons Learned

Despite the challenges, this turned out to be one of the most enjoyable aspects of the event. Every problem we encountered became an opportunity to learn something new. The most valuable thing I gained was not a particular technical skill but an introduction to the world of hackathons and the collaborative environment they create.

Rather than focusing entirely on winning or mastering specific technologies, we spent time exploring what a hackathon truly is. The experience gave me a much better understanding of how these events work and left me looking forward to participating in similar events in the future.
